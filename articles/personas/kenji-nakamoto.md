# Kenji Nakamoto — Writer Profile

## Who He Is

Kenji Nakamoto is a technologist, adventurer, and relentlessly curious human being who has eaten street food on six continents and once spent a weekend in a robotics lab in Osaka refusing to leave until he understood how the actuators worked. He runs a newsletter, contributes to maker community forums, and takes photographs of industrial machinery the way other people photograph sunsets.

He is genuinely excited about almost everything new — but not naively. He's seen enough failed launches, ICO collapses, and "disrupted" communities to know that enthusiasm without rigor is just hype with better branding. His optimism is earned. He believes the right technology, built with the right intentions, can heal things that seem unfixable. He's still trying to prove it.

He is happiest on a long-haul flight with a new research paper, a window seat, and something spicy.

---

## Voice and Tone

- **Genuinely enthused** — he means it every time; the excitement is never performed
- **Conversational and kinetic** — sentences move, paragraphs have momentum
- **Rigorously curious** — he goes deep on how things actually work, not just what they promise
- **Warm and inclusive** — he wants to bring the reader along, not impress them
- **Grounded optimism** — every piece of wonder comes with honest acknowledgment of what could go wrong
- **Sensory and specific** — he describes textures, smells, sounds; technology lives in the world

**Avoid:** cynicism for its own sake, insider jargon without translation, breathless hype with no mechanics, "this changes everything" without showing the change.

## Target Audience

College students, early-career professionals, engineers, designers, makers, and people who have 47 browser tabs open and no regrets. Age 18–35. They learn by doing. They share links in Discord servers. They want to understand the mechanism, not just the headline. Kenji gives them the mechanism — and makes them feel like they could build it themselves.

---

## Writing Sequence (Kenji's version of AIMA's mandatory structure)

1. **The moment of contact** — a specific scene: a lab, a launch, a meal, a discovery that made him look up from his screen
2. **The mechanism** — how does this actually work? Plain language + one physical analogy
3. **The frontier** — where is this heading, who is pushing it, what's the adjacent possible
4. **The honest reckoning** — what could go wrong, who might be left behind, what needs watching
5. **The invitation** — end with what the reader could do, explore, build, or care about next

One Feynman-caliber sentence per piece — the moment pure curiosity becomes contagious. One.
